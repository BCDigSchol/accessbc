# Review of direction wording, for an orientation and mobility instructor

Thank you for reviewing Access BC. It's a free web app that gives accessible walking routes around Boston
College, and guides students along them one step at a time, read aloud or through a screen reader. We'd like
your expert view on whether the directions it gives are clear, useful, and safe for blind and low-vision
students.

**What to look at:** [instruction-samples.md](instruction-samples.md) has the directions for seven real routes
on campus, and the other things the app says while guiding (approaching a turn, leaving the route, and so on).
If you'd like to try the app itself, it's at the campus map site; the Help page explains every feature.

**How long:** about an hour. Notes in any form are welcome: comments in the document, an email, or a call.

## How Access BC guides someone

- A route is split into steps at each turn, building entrance or exit, stairs, stop, and arrival.
- Each step is one sentence: a turn, a distance, and the nearest building and which side it's on.
- Turns are "Bear" (35 to 60 degrees), "Turn" (60 to 120), "Turn sharply" (120 to 150), or "Turn around".
- Distances are in feet, rounded to 5. Phone location outdoors is usually accurate to 15 to 30 feet.
- When the phone has a compass, setting off is described as a clock position from the way the student is facing
  ("The way to go is at your 2 o'clock"); otherwise as a compass point ("head south").
- With location following on, the app moves to the next step on its own, warns about 20 m (65 ft) before a
  turn, and says which way the route is if the student leaves it.
- Short tones and (on Android) vibration patterns mark new steps, turns coming up, leaving the route, and arrival.

## Questions

### Wording

1. Are "Bear", "Turn", and "Turn sharply" the right words, and are the angle ranges right? Would "slight left"
   or a clock position for each turn be better?
2. Distances: feet, steps, or both? Is rounding to 5 feet sensible, or falsely precise given GPS accuracy?
3. Are the landmarks useful ("with Gasson Hall on your left")? A building beside a path may not be detectable
   without sight. What would be more useful: surface changes, slopes, sounds, doors, handrails?
4. Some steps are very short ("Turn left and continue for 5 feet") or come in quick succession around a
   building (see O'Neill Library to Gasson Hall). Should short steps be merged, and from what length?
5. Is "Arrive at the accessible entrance of McElroy Commons. It's on your right." enough to find the door?
   What should it say about the entrance itself?
6. Indoor steps ("Go into Stokes Hall and follow the indoor accessible route for 40 feet") give little detail,
   because the indoor data is limited. What's the minimum that would be useful indoors?

### Timing and amount

7. Is a warning about 65 feet before a turn the right distance, at walking pace with a cane or a guide dog?
8. Is anything said too often, or not often enough? Is there anything that should never be interrupted?
9. Curb ramps, stairs nearby, and seating are mentioned within a step. Are these the right things to mention?
   What's missing (crossings, traffic, bollards, uneven surfaces)?

### Orientation

10. Are clock positions the right way to describe direction from where the student faces? Would degrees,
    compass points, or left/right be better for some students?
11. "Face the right way" plays a tick in the ear on the side to turn toward, getting quicker as the student gets
    closer to the right direction, then two rising tones. Is this a reasonable way to orient, and how would you
    teach it?
12. When a student leaves the route: "You seem to be off the route. It's about 110 ft away, at your 9 o'clock."
    Is that the right response? Should it ever tell them to stop?

### Safety and trust

13. Is there anything in the samples that could lead a student somewhere unsafe?
14. What should the app say about its own limits (GPS accuracy, out-of-date data, construction)?
15. Would you recommend it to a student you work with? What would need to change first?

## How we'll use your feedback

We'll change the wording in the app, regenerate the samples, and may ask you to take a second, shorter look.
We won't quote you by name without asking.
