# Session guide: one-to-one testing with a student

About an hour: 10 minutes to start, 20 minutes of tasks seated, 20 minutes walking a short route, 10 minutes to
finish. One facilitator leads and walks beside the participant; a second person (optional) takes notes on the
[observation sheet](observation-sheet.csv).

Choose tasks to fit the participant: tasks marked **(walk)** are outdoors; low-vision participants also do the
display tasks; wheelchair users skip "Face the right way" unless they want to try it.

## Before the participant arrives

- [ ] Walked the test route today: no construction, entrances open, the start and end doors confirmed.
- [ ] Backup phones charged, with the site open, and settings reset (Accessibility settings to defaults).
- [ ] Consent form ready in the participant's preferred format; payment ready.
- [ ] Observation sheet open, with a new row per task.

**Suggested route:** O'Neill Library to Gasson Hall (about 550 ft, mostly level, several turns), or Gasson Hall
to McElroy Commons (about 1,250 ft, with an indoor stretch through Stokes Hall) for a longer walk. Check
[instruction-samples.md](instruction-samples.md) for the current directions.

## Introduction (read or adapt)

> Thanks for helping. We're testing Access BC, a campus map that gives accessible directions. We're testing the
> app, not you: if something is hard, that's the app's problem and exactly what we need to find. Please think
> out loud as you go: what you expect, what you hear, what's confusing. You can stop or skip anything at any time.
>
> For the walk, I'll be right beside you the whole time. Your own judgement and mobility skills come first. If
> the app ever tells you to do something that doesn't feel right, don't do it, and tell me.

Go through consent. Ask permission to record audio. Then ask:

- Which phone and screen reader (or zoom, magnifier, large text) do you use? Which voice and speed?
- How do you usually find your way to a building you haven't been to on campus?
- Have you used apps for directions before (Google Maps, BlindSquare, Soundscape, Lazarus, others)? What do you
  like and dislike about them?

## Tasks, seated

Give each task in words; don't show how. Note on the sheet: completed alone, with help, or not completed;
time; what they said; where they got stuck.

1. **Find out about a building.** "Find out whether Fulton Hall has an accessible entrance, and where it is."
   (Search, then the building information.)
2. **Get directions.** "Get directions from O'Neill Library to Gasson Hall, and find out how long the walk is."
3. **Review the route.** "Without starting the walk, go through the directions. What do you think of them?"
4. **Settings.** "Set the app up the way you'd want it." (Watch whether they find Accessibility, and which
   settings they choose: I use a screen reader, Read aloud, clock positions, sounds, dark mode, larger text.)
5. **Add a stop.** "On the way, you'd like to stop at a single-use restroom. Add one to your trip."
6. **Help.** "Find out what the sounds during guidance mean." (Help page; do they use Listen?)

Low-vision participants also:

7. **Display.** "Make the map and directions as easy as possible for you to see." (Dark mode, larger text,
   bigger symbols, zoom, large step-by-step view.)

## Tasks, walking (walk)

Walk to the start building first. The facilitator stays beside the participant and doesn't help with the app
unless asked or unless safety requires it.

8. **Start guidance.** "Start the directions to Gasson Hall and follow them." (Audio guidance or step-by-step;
   which do they choose? If on an iPhone, note what happens with the motion permission prompt.)
9. **Orient.** At the start: "Which way do you need to go?" (Face the right way, clock position, or their own
   strategy.)
10. **Where am I?** Partway along: "Find out where you are and what's nearby."
11. **Off route.** If it happens naturally, observe. Otherwise, with consent, the facilitator suggests a short
    detour of about 50 ft: "Let's step off the path here. What does the app tell you? Get back on route."
12. **Arrive.** "Find the accessible entrance." (Note whether arrival information is enough to find the door.)

Watch for: GPS lag or jumps, steps advancing too early or late, sounds or speech the participant didn't notice
(traffic noise, earbuds), the phone locking, the participant having to stop to use the phone, and their trust:
are they following the app or their own judgement?

## Questions at the end

- What was most useful? What was most frustrating?
- Were the directions worded the way you'd want? Clock positions, distances, landmarks?
- Were the sounds and vibration helpful? Was there too much speech, or too little?
- Would you use Access BC on your own to get somewhere new on campus? What would need to change first?
- Is there anything you expected it to do that it didn't?
- On a scale of 1 to 7, how easy was it to use overall? How much did you trust the directions?

Thank them, give payment, and ask whether they'd be willing to try a later version.

## Afterwards

Complete the observation sheet the same day. Log problems as GitHub issues labelled `usability`, with severity
(critical, serious, minor), and without names or identifying details. See [README.md](README.md).
